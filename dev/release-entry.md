## 83.1.0 ({{STAMP}}): a press is never handed to another player

- **WHAT THE OWNER SAID.** *"still doesn't work I should be able to swipe away from recents and hit the widgit play and it should play"*, after installing 83.0.9.
- **WHAT WAS STILL WRONG.** 83.0.9 fixed the resume itself, but not the road the press took to reach it. Whether a press went to the hidden player was decided by a "heartbeat" timestamp written only when the page happens to push widget state - so an app killed up to fifteen seconds earlier still looked alive, and the press was handed back to the provider. The provider's answer to a play is `AudioManager.dispatchMediaKeyEvent`, a key to the **whole phone**: it starts whatever player the system saw last (Spotify), and it reaches nothing at all when there is no session. The hidden player was only started after 2.5 s of that, so a press could be spent entirely on a player that was not this one - no sound, no app opened.
- **THE FIX.** The three real cases are separate now. Music already playing means the press is a **pause**, and only that case goes back to the provider. Alive but silent means the **running page** is asked to resume - it already polls for exactly this every ten seconds - and gets twelve seconds to answer before the hidden player takes over. Nothing alive means the **hidden player starts immediately**, with no media key anywhere in the path. It also gives up in about nine seconds instead of forty, so a press that cannot play ends in the app, and the button gives one 70 ms buzz so it is obvious whether the press reached the app at all.
- **THE PAGE HALF.** The resume no longer refuses to start when the saved queue did not survive a relaunch: the song comes from the saved playback state, so a lost queue can no longer make every retry return false without touching a song. Every dead end says why, once per reason, instead of failing silently: the trail reads `page -> no play: "<song>" has no audio file` rather than nothing at all.
- **WHAT WAS CHECKED.** `python3 dev/patch-8310.py --check` exits **1** before the payload and **0** after it, a second run is a no-op, the page lands at 83.1.0 with a new cache name and changelog entry, and all seven inline scripts still parse. The native chain ran end to end on a sandbox Android project - `patch-widget.py`, `dev/widget-sizes.py`, `dev/ghost-host.py`, `dev/ghost-route.py` - with balanced braces, a well-formed manifest, the VIBRATE permission added, the old media-key routing provably gone, and `dev/ghost-route.py` a no-op on a tree it has already routed. The rebuilt bundles reached their fixed point in the release commit: {{ROOT}} bytes on the full channel and {{PLAY}} on the store build.
- **NOT CLAIMED.** No phone ran this, and I could not press the button here. What is proven from the code: a resume can no longer be turned into a system-wide media key, so it cannot start another app; it starts the hidden player at once when nothing is alive; and a press that cannot play opens the app within about nine seconds instead of doing nothing. What is still a device question is whether an alpha-0 WebView window is allowed to keep audio alive at all. If it is not, the trail says `player -> no audio in 9 s, opening the app` and the press still ends in the app.

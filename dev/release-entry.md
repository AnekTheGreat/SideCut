## 83.5.0 ({{STAMP}}): A second deck, for mixes you mix yourself

- **WHAT WAS MISSING.** DJ Mode has carried a second audio lane (`deckEngineB`) since the crossfade work, but only the automatic "crossfade to next" ever drove it: there was no way to load a song onto it, start it yourself, or hold two songs in the air at once. Everything that made the mode feel like a record player - the platter, the scratch, the loop, the hot cues, the EQ and the pads - acted on one deck only, so the only mix you could make was the one the app performed for you.
- **WHAT THIS ADDS.** A mix panel inside DJ Mode: deck B with its own load (previous/next in the current playlist), play/pause, cue, pitch (up to ±16%), gain (±12 dB), and a hand crossfader with an **equal-power curve**, so the middle of the fader is as loud as either end instead of sagging. The automatic next-song fade is untouched; this is the fader you perform with.
- **THE TEMPO IS READ, NOT TYPED.** `scDetectTempo` builds an onset envelope (the positive rise in short-window loudness) and finds the beat spacing it repeats at most strongly between 60 and 190 BPM, then takes the phase from the first strong onset. BEAT SYNC matches deck B's rate to what deck A is actually playing, and schedules B to start on one of its own beats exactly when A's next beat lands - a hand mix stays in time instead of drifting apart.
- **THE PICTURE.** Both songs are drawn as waveforms with their beat grid and both playheads; dragging on the drawing jogs deck B forwards or backwards. HAND OVER TO B makes B the turntable the knobs, pads and hot cues control, so a set can be mixed straight through, and the existing recorder still captures the master output, both decks included.
- **CHECKS.** `dev/patch-8350.py --check` exits 1 before the payload and 0 after it, a second run is a no-op, all seven inline scripts of the patched page parse, every element id the engine touches exists in the markup, and the engine is driven in the session that wrote it: the tempo reader returns 127.7 BPM for a synthesised 128 BPM click track, 89.6 for 90, and 100.0 for 100; the fader's two gains sum to 1.000 at the middle and resolve to exactly A and exactly B at the ends; BEAT SYNC sets a 128-vs-96 pair to a 1.319 rate and lands B's start 0.23 s out, against 0.221 s of expected phase. Half-time readings are possible on very fast material (174 BPM reads as 87), which still lands the grid on beats. Rebuilt bundles: {{ROOT}} bytes on the full channel, {{PLAY}} on the store build.
- **NOT CLAIMED.** No phone ran here and nothing was listened to. What is proven is the arithmetic and the wiring; the feel of a mix, the latency of a real device and the sound of two songs against each other are things only the owner can judge.

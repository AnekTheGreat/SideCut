## 83 ({{STAMP}}): the speed sits in the song's settings, and the mixes keep it

- **WHAT THE OWNER SAID.** *"why is the stupid thing to increase speed there it should be in song settings and it should be like only for that song and also apply for when you download mixes in SideCut change both versions"*. Both builds come from the one `index.html`; the store build is the same file with `SC_IS_PLAY` on.
- **THE CONTROL IN THE OBVIOUS PLACE WAS THE ONLY ONE THAT WAS NOT PER SONG.** 81.8 kept a rate per song in `sidecut.song.speed` and drew it twice: a chip on the now bar and a rate row in the Studio sheet. The song's own ··· menu still carried the old `Playback speed` slider, wired to `setPlaybackSpeed` - the whole player's rate - so setting a speed where a person looks for a song's settings changed every song, and the per-song control sat on a bar that shows no other per-song setting.
- **THE ANSWER.** That slider now writes `scSpeedSet(t.id, …)`, prints "· this song only", shows the exact rate, and carries a Reset that appears the moment the song is off 1x. The now-bar chip is gone, and `scSpeedChipAttach` clears any chip an older page left in the bar. `setPlaybackSpeed` no longer repaints the sheet's slider (it is the song's now) and applies `scSpeedFor(scSpeedPlayingId())`, so the global rate, the Studio rate and the song's own rate stack in one place instead of three.
- **THE MIXES KEEP IT.** `downloadCrossfadedMix` decoded each song and blended it untouched, so a mix of sped-up songs came out at 1x. `scStretchBuffer` now stretches each buffer by its own rate before the blend: grains of ~46ms copied apart at the ratio, each placed where it best continues the one before (WSOLA), cross-faded with a Hann window. It is the offline twin of the element's `preservesPitch`; a rate of 1 returns the buffer untouched, so a mix of untouched songs is byte for byte what it was, and a stretch that throws returns the buffer rather than losing the song.
- **BOTH VERSIONS, ASKED FOR IN THE SAME BREATH.** 83 names itself in `APP_VERSION`, the changelog entry above it, and `sidecut-shell-v83`, and both OTA channels are rebuilt from the same page: `ota/` for the full build and `ota-play/` for the store build, the flag baked into the Play zip as always.
- **WHAT WAS CHECKED.** `python3 dev/patch-83.py --check` exited **1** before the payload and **0** after it, and a second run was a no-op; every inline script in the page compiles. `node dev/check-83.cjs` drives the new code rather than grepping it, 33 checks over a 440Hz tone: at 1.5x, 2x, 0.75x and 0.5x the stretch comes back at 440Hz (pitch kept), at the right length, with the two channels in step and no NaN or silence, 1x returns the very same buffer, a garbage rate does not throw, and the sheet's slider is shown writing the song's rate while the now bar is shown carrying no chip. Both channels reached their fixed point at {{ROOT}} bytes for the full page and {{PLAY}} for the store build.
- **NOT CLAIMED.** No phone and no headphones ran here: no stretch was listened to and no mix was downloaded on a device, so what a 1.5x mix sounds like through a car speaker is still unheard. What is proven is the code the controls call, and the pitch and length it produces, on the bytes the release ships.

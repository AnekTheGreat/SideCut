## 83.4.0 ({{STAMP}}): The app answers the headphones itself

- **WHAT WAS STILL WRONG.** 83.3.0 fixed the sound half of a press. The press itself still went through the media-session plugin, and that plugin's service advertises a transport action only when it holds a live `PluginCall` for it - it builds its `PlaybackState` actions from that same list (`MediaSessionService.update()` walks `plugin.hasActionHandler`). A player that is not armed - bridge not ready at that moment, the opt-in notification switch off, or the crash recorder having switched it off once - therefore publishes a session that declares **no actions at all**, and a phone does not route a button to a session that says it can do nothing. Pause *and* unpause then reach nobody, on every headset at once, while the in-app button keeps working because it never leaves the page.
- **THE FIX, NATIVE HALF.** `SideCutKeys.java` (dev/sidecut-keys.java.txt, written by dev/native-keys.py) is a media session the app owns rather than a plugin: created with `MainActivity` in `onCreate`, kept `setActive(true)`, and always declaring play, pause, play/pause, stop, next and previous. It reads the key event by name - a single earbud press is `KEYCODE_MEDIA_PLAY_PAUSE` or `HEADSETHOOK` - and forwards one command into the WebView that made the sound. `GhostPlayerActivity` extends `MainActivity`, so `attach` refuses anything that is not exactly `MainActivity`: a hidden player must not steal the keys.
- **THE FIX, PAGE HALF.** `window.__scNativeKey(cmd)` (dev/keys-8340.js.txt) is that door: `toggle` asks the page whether a song is running and either pauses or goes through the 83.3.0 `scTransportPlay`, `next` and `previous` follow the same rule every player uses (past three seconds, previous means the top of this song), and `pause` stops the deck. The page reports `playing`/`paused` back to the native session through the `SideCutKeys` bridge, because the session a phone routes a press to is the one it believes is playing.
- **CHECKS.** `dev/patch-8340.py --check` exits 1 before the payload and 0 after it, a second run is a no-op, all seven inline scripts of the patched page parse, and the key door is driven in the session that wrote it: a playing element toggles to pause, a paused element goes through `scTransportPlay`, next advances, previous past three seconds rewinds, previous before three seconds moves back a song, a press with no player is reported instead of dropped, and the state reaches the bridge the native side listens on. The native half is run twice on a stand-in `MainActivity` and is idempotent. Rebuilt bundles: {{ROOT}} bytes on the full channel, {{PLAY}} on the store build.
- **NOT CLAIMED.** No phone and no earbuds ran here. What is proven is that the press now has an owner that always exists and always advertises actions, and that every command from it lands on the right page function. The APK is the half that carries it: the page alone cannot answer a button the phone is not routing.

## 83.0.3 ({{STAMP}}): a paused song answers the headphones and the widget again

- **WHAT THE OWNER SAID.** *"in SideCut if I haven't played a song in my earbuds for 20 minutes it should still play when I hit unpause in my earbuds, in the widgit I shouldn't have to open the app, stuff like this fix because Spotify can do it why cant you"*.
- **WHAT WAS ACTUALLY WRONG.** Ten minutes after a pause with the app away, `armMediaRelease()` did four things: stopped the media heartbeat, gave the wake lock back, dropped the deck's foreground hold, and pushed one truthful `paused` state. Then it stopped. That single push was the last thing that session ever heard: nothing refreshed the card, and the entry the headphones' play button - or the widget's - has to land on stopped being reliable. Twenty minutes later the earbud press arrived at a session Android had dropped, so it came out silent, and the only way back was to open the app. The widget's own fallback is the same symptom wearing a different coat: `SideCutWidgetProvider` sends the key and, when nothing is playing and that key finds no session, calls `askResume()` + `openApp()` - the reported "it opens the app instead of playing".
- **THE ANSWER.** Parked no longer goes quiet. `startParkedBeat(track)` keeps the entry, its metadata and its controls warm for as long as a song sits on the deck: one bridge call a minute, no wake lock, no decoding, no position churn - the paused notification the phone was already showing, restated so it cannot go stale underneath a press. The controls are re-armed every fourth beat rather than every beat, because re-registering them is a bridge call and a session is rarely rebuilt more often than that. A press of any kind goes through `scTransportPress()`, which already cancels the release clock, clears the parked flag and re-arms the handlers; `rearmMediaSession()` now also stops the beat, so the deck's own heartbeat owns the session again the moment the song comes back. When there is genuinely nothing on the deck the session is still released outright (`'none'`), exactly as before - a queue that ended, or an app with no song, still gets its battery back.
- **THE SETTINGS NOTE SAID THE OPPOSITE.** The Widget tab carried "if the app is closed or swiped away from recents, the widget will not play the right audio - open SideCut again to get the widget back in sync". It now says what actually happens: a paused song stays reachable from the widget, the lock screen and the headphones, and the one case that still needs the app is a phone that has killed SideCut.
- **HOW IT WAS DONE.** `dev/patch-8303.py`, applied by `release.yml`: it asserts each anchor appears exactly once before replacing it, bumps `APP_VERSION` to 83.0.3, writes the changelog entry above 83.0.2's, moves the service worker cache to `sidecut-shell-v83.0.3` so a phone that already has 83.0.2 cannot keep serving its own offline copy, and leaves every other byte alone.
- **WHAT WAS CHECKED.** `python3 dev/patch-8303.py --check` exited **1** before the payload and **0** after it; a second run printed "already on the page" and `sha256sum` showed the page and the service worker byte for byte the same. `node dev/check-8303.cjs` lifts `startParkedBeat`/`stopParkedBeat` out of the page and drives them against a fake clock: one beat on park, at 60000 ms, exempt from the battery saver; a beat restates the parked song and `paused`; four beats are four pushes with the controls re-armed only on the first and the fifth; clearing the parked flag ends the beat; a park with nothing on the deck ends itself; the grace window hands its resumable song to the beat and still releases outright when there is none; `rearmMediaSession` stops the beat; the transport press cancels the window and re-arms; every inline script compiles; the patched page boots in jsdom and settings still opens with its nine tabs. {{ROOT}} bytes on the full channel and {{PLAY}} on the store build; both OTA bundles reached their fixed point in the release commit.
- **NOT CLAIMED.** No phone and no earbuds ran here, and nothing was listened to: whether a particular Android build keeps the notification alive with this beat, and whether a given headset sends play as `play` or as `playpause`, is the phone's business. What is proven is the code the press lands on - a session that is stated once a minute instead of once, and the two places that start and stop it - on the page the release ships. This is a page fix, so it arrives over the air; no install is needed for it.
